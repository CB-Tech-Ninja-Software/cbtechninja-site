Uploading a HEIC photo: get a JPEG under the size limit

Photos from many phones are saved as HEIC, and some forms are fussy about file type and size. Here is how FitSize turns one into a JPEG that fits.

What FitSize does with a photo

  • Accepts JPEG, PNG, HEIC and other common image types.
  • Always saves the result as a JPEG.
  • Scales the photo down and compresses it only as much as needed to get under your limit.
  • Leaves a photo that is already under the limit as it is, and tells you “Already under limit”.
  • Keeps the photo the right way up, and turns transparent areas white.

Steps

  1. Check what the form accepts. The upload page tells you the allowed types and the size limit. Use those, not guesses.
  2. Drop the HEIC photo into FitSize and choose the limit (or Custom, such as 750 KB).
  3. Click Fit, then Save. You get a -fit JPEG copy.

What FitSize doesn't do

FitSize works to a file-size limit. It doesn't crop or resize to exact pixel dimensions, so it isn't a tool for forms that require a specific width and height in pixels (for example some ID-photo requirements). Check the form's rules for those.

Why the quality changes

A smaller limit means a smaller or more compressed image. FitSize tries the gentlest settings that fit, so a 5 MB limit costs little and a 200 KB limit costs more. If the limit is so small that the photo would be unreadable, FitSize says so instead of producing it: see when a limit is too small.
