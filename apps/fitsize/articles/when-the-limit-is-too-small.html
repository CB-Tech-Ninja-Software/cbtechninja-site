When a size limit is too small: what to try

Some limits can't be reached without ruining the file. FitSize tells you instead of giving you something unreadable. Here is what to do next.

What the message means

If FitSize can't get under your limit without making the file unreadable, it says so plainly and shows the smallest size it managed. That number is useful: it tells you how far off you are.

Options

  • Try a larger limit. Often the form allows more than you assumed. Re-read the upload page.
  • Combine less. A PDF with many scanned pages is big because each page is an image. If the portal accepts separate files, upload them separately and fit each one.
  • Re-export from the source. If you made the PDF yourself, exporting at a lower image quality from the original program may give FitSize a smaller starting point.
  • Ask the portal. If the limit really is too small for a legitimate document, contact the organisation's support.

PDFs and searchable text

For PDFs, FitSize tries to keep text selectable first. Only if that can't reach the limit will it rebuild pages as images, and the result screen tells you when the text in the copy won't be selectable. If you need searchable text, a larger limit is the way to keep it.

Check before you upload

  • Open the copy and look at every page or the whole photo.
  • Confirm it is under the limit in your file browser.
  • Keep your original: FitSize never changes it.
