KB vs MB: how file-size limits are counted

Why a file that looks like 2 MB can still be rejected by a 2 MB limit, and how FitSize avoids the problem.

Two ways to count

There are two conventions in everyday use. In the decimal convention, 1 KB is 1,000 bytes and 1 MB is 1,000,000 bytes. In the binary convention, 1 KB is 1,024 bytes and 1 MB is 1,048,576 bytes. Different operating systems, apps and websites use different ones, which is how a file can look like it is under a limit on your computer and over it on the portal.

The arithmetic

LimitDecimal bytesBinary bytes
200 KB200,000204,800
2 MB2,000,0002,097,152
5 MB5,000,0005,242,880

The decimal figure is always the smaller one. A file under 2,000,000 bytes is under 2 MB by either convention.

What FitSize does

FitSize uses decimal units on purpose, so the copy it gives you passes whichever way the portal counts. It also measures the real size of the finished file instead of estimating it.

Practical tips

  • If a file is only just under the limit and still gets rejected, choose a slightly lower limit, such as 1.9 MB for a 2 MB form.
  • Type custom limits with a unit: 750 KB, 1.5 MB.
