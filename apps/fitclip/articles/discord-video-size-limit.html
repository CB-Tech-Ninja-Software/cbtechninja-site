How to send a video that is over Discord's size limit

A clip that won't upload isn't stuck: you can make it smaller. Here is how to work out the size you need and fit the video to it.

Check the current limit first

Discord's upload cap can differ by account type and can change over time, so don't rely on a number you read in a forum. Check Discord's own help centre for the current figure: support.discord.com. We are deliberately not quoting a number here because we could not verify one against Discord's pages while writing this guide.

FitSize Video's Discord preset is 10 MB. That is a convenient starting point, but if your account's limit is different, choose Custom and type it in (for example 25 MB).

Step by step

  1. Drop the video into FitSize Video. MP4, MOV and M4V are accepted.
  2. Choose the limit. Pick the Discord preset or enter your own with Custom.
  3. Click Fit. FitSize Video works out a bitrate for your clip's length, encodes an MP4 and measures the real size. If it lands over, it retries at a lower bitrate.
  4. Save the copy. Upload that file to Discord. Your original is untouched.

What to expect for quality

Smaller files mean lower quality, and the shorter your clip, the better it looks at a given size. A 20-second clip at 10 MB looks much better than a 5-minute one at 10 MB because the same bytes cover far fewer seconds. If you only need part of a recording, cut it down before you fit it: FitSize Video does not trim, so do that in another tool first.

If the clip is simply too long, FitSize Video says “too long for this size”. See why a video won't fit a size limit for what the numbers look like.

Why not use an online compressor?

Online tools make you upload the whole video first. FitSize Video does the work on your Mac with no network access, so a private clip stays private.
