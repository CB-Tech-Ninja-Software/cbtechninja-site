Email attachment size limits: check yours and fit a video to it

Every email provider caps attachment size. Here is what we could verify, how to find your own limit, and how to fit a video under it.

What we can verify

  • Personal Gmail accounts: Google's help page says “For personal Gmail accounts, the limit is 25 MB.”
  • Work and school Google accounts: the Google Workspace administrator sets the sending and receiving limits, so there is no single number. Ask your administrator.

Both points are from Google's Gmail help page on attachment limits. For other providers (Outlook, Yahoo, iCloud, your employer's server) check that provider's help pages, because we have not verified their numbers and they change.

The recipient's limit matters too

A message has to get through the sender's limit and the recipient's. If you aren't sure what the other side allows, aim a little lower than your own limit, or use a size you know they can receive.

Fit the video with FitSize Video

  1. Drop the video in.
  2. Choose Email (25 MB) or Custom with the size you need, for example 10 MB.
  3. Click Fit and Save. FitSize Video measures the finished file, so what you attach is really under the limit.

FitSize Video's sizes use decimal units (1 MB = 1,000,000 bytes). That is the safer way to count: a file under 25 MB by that count is also under 25 MB if a service counts in 1,048,576-byte units.

If the clip is long

A long video at a small limit can't look good. FitSize Video will say it is too long for that size. Your options are a larger limit, a shorter clip, or sharing a link instead of an attachment. See why a video won't fit a size limit.
