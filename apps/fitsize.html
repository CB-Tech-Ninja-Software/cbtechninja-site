FitSize

For anyone staring at an upload form that says “file too large”: job applications, visa and school portals, government forms. Pick the limit, drop in the PDF or photo, and get a copy that fits, without uploading it anywhere.

The problem: the form says 2 MB and your file is 6 MB

Upload portals set a size limit and reject anything over it. The usual fixes are awkward: an online compressor means uploading your passport scan or CV to a stranger's server, and a generic compressor won't tell you whether the result is actually under the limit.

  • You need a specific number (200 KB, 500 KB, 2 MB, 5 MB), not a vague “smaller”.
  • The documents are often personal: IDs, transcripts, CVs, tax forms.
  • You want to know the copy really is under the limit before you try the upload again.

How it works

  1. Drop a PDF or a photo onto the window, or click Choose file.
  2. Pick a limit: 200 KB, 500 KB, 1 MB, 2 MB, 5 MB, or type your own such as 750 KB or 1.5 MB.
  3. Click Fit, then Save. Your original is never changed.

FitSize measures the real size of the result, so the copy it gives you is under your limit. Read the full walk-through.

Features

  • Target size first. Presets from 200 KB to 5 MB, or any size you type.
  • Photos: JPEG, PNG, HEIC and other common image types, saved as a JPEG copy, scaled and compressed only as much as needed.
  • PDFs: images inside are recompressed and text stays selectable. If that isn't enough, it rebuilds pages as images and warns you first.
  • Before and after sizes on the result screen, so you can see what you got.
  • Honest when it can't: if a limit is too small, it says so and shows the smallest size it reached.
  • Private by design. No accounts, ads, analytics, tracking or network access.

Good to know

  • Sizes use decimal units (1 MB = 1,000,000 bytes), so a file under 2 MB is under 2 MB by either common counting method.
  • Output is a copy with -fit added to the name, saved where you choose.
  • Photos are always saved as JPEG; transparent areas become white.
  • It does not edit photos to exact pixel dimensions. One file at a time.

Frequently asked questions

Does FitSize change my original file?

No. It makes a copy, named with -fit added, saved where you choose. The original stays untouched.

What files does it take?

Photos (JPEG, PNG, HEIC and other common image types) and PDFs. One file at a time.

Will my PDF text still be selectable?

FitSize first recompresses the images inside the PDF and keeps your text selectable. If that isn't enough to reach your limit, it falls back to rebuilding each page as an image, and warns you first: in that case text in the smaller copy cannot be selected or searched. Page count and page size stay the same.

What format do photos come out as?

Always JPEG. Transparent areas become white. A photo already under your limit is left as it is.

What if my limit is too small?

FitSize tells you plainly and shows the smallest size it managed, so you can try a larger limit rather than getting an unreadable file.

Can it resize a photo to exact pixel dimensions, like for a passport photo?

No. FitSize works to a file-size limit, not to exact pixel dimensions.

Does anything get uploaded?

No. FitSize has no accounts, ads, analytics, tracking or network access. Everything happens on your Mac.

How much is it and when is it out?

FitSize is in development and not yet on the Mac App Store. Price and release date have not been announced.

What does it need?

macOS 14 or later.
