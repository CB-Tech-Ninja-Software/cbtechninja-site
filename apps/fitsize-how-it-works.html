How FitSize works

What happens between dropping a PDF or photo on FitSize and getting a copy that is guaranteed under your limit.

1. Pick the limit the form asks for

The limit picker offers 200 KB, 500 KB, 1 MB, 2 MB and 5 MB, or Custom: type 750 KB or 1.5 MB and it is checked as you type. Sizes use decimal units (1 MB = 1,000,000 bytes), so a copy under 2 MB is under 2 MB however the portal counts.

2. Photos: scale, then compress, only as much as needed

Photos (JPEG, PNG, HEIC and other common types) are saved as a JPEG copy. If the photo is already under your limit it is left as it is. Otherwise FitSize tries gentler settings first and steps down to a smaller size only when it has to, so you keep as much quality as the limit allows. The photo's orientation is preserved, and transparent areas become white.

3. PDFs: keep the text if possible

For PDFs, FitSize first recompresses the images inside the document and keeps your text selectable. The result screen says so: “Text kept, images compressed”. Page count and page size stay the same.

4. The fallback, with a warning

If recompressing the images can't reach your limit, FitSize can rebuild each page as an image instead. The result screen tells you when this happens, because in that case text in the smaller copy cannot be selected or searched. If you need searchable text, try a larger limit.

5. The real size is measured

FitSize doesn't estimate. It measures the finished file, so the copy it hands you really is under your limit.

6. A plain answer when a limit is too small

If the limit is too small to reach without making the file unreadable, FitSize says so and shows the smallest size it managed, so you can try a larger limit.

7. Original untouched, nothing uploaded

FitSize saves a copy with -fit added to the name where you choose. It has no accounts, ads, analytics, tracking or network access, so your documents never leave your device.
