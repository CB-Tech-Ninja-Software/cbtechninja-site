How FitClip works

What happens between dropping a video on FitClip and getting a file that fits.

1. You choose the size, not the settings

Most video tools ask for bitrate, resolution and codec. FitClip asks one question: how big can the file be? Choose Discord (10 MB), WhatsApp (16 MB) or Email (25 MB), or type your own limit.

Presets are a convenience. Services change their limits from time to time, so use Custom if yours is different.

2. A bitrate budget from your clip's length

Video size is mostly bitrate multiplied by duration, so FitClip divides your limit by the clip's length. It reserves a share of the limit for the file container and for rate-control overshoot, and sets aside 96 kbps for AAC audio. What is left is the video budget.

That is why short clips look better at a given size than long ones: the same number of bytes is spread over fewer seconds.

3. Resolution chosen so the picture holds up

FitClip picks an output resolution so there are enough bits per pixel for the picture to stay watchable. A tighter limit means a lower resolution rather than a smear of compression blocks. The result screen reports what it used, for example the codec, bitrate and frame size.

4. Re-encoded to MP4 with H.264 and AAC

The clip is transcoded with the video technology built into macOS into an MP4 with H.264 video and AAC audio. A progress bar shows how far along it is, and you can cancel at any time.

5. Measured, then retried if needed

Estimates can be off, so FitClip measures the real size of the finished file. If it is over your limit, it retries with a lower video bitrate, up to three times, until it fits.

6. An honest answer when it can't be done

If a clip is too long for a limit and the video would drop below a watchable bitrate, FitClip says “too long for this size” instead of handing you something unwatchable. Choose a larger limit, or use a shorter clip.

7. Your original stays untouched, and stays on your Mac

FitClip writes a new file, with -fit added to the name where you save it. It has no accounts, ads, analytics, tracking or network access, so your video is never uploaded.
