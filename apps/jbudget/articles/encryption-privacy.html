Why Your Budget Should Be Private: jBudget's Encryption Approach

How jBudget keeps your financial data private and what that means for your security.

Why This Matters

Your budget contains sensitive information: account balances, income details, debt amounts, payment schedules. This is exactly the kind of data that should never leave your device.

Many budgeting apps sync your data to the cloud for backup or multi-device access. That convenience comes with a cost: your financial data passes through someone else's servers, where it could be intercepted, stored, or subpoenaed.

jBudget takes a different approach: your data stays on your device, encrypted.

The Cloud Tradeoff

Cloud-based budgeting apps offer real advantages:

  • Sync across devices: Check your budget on your phone, update it on your laptop
  • Automatic backup: Your data is safe even if your device fails
  • Easier sharing: Partner accounts and collaborative planning

But they come with privacy costs:

  • Data on someone else's servers: Your budget data is stored in data centers you don't control
  • Legal exposure: Your data can be subpoenaed in litigation, or accessed by government request
  • Breach risk: If a company's security fails, your financial data is at risk
  • Business model alignment: Some apps monetize user data through anonymized analytics or sell insights to third parties

How jBudget Works: Local-First Encryption

jBudget runs on your device (iPhone, iPad, Mac or Windows, with Android in open testing) and stores everything locally. No cloud sync. No servers holding your data. Your entire budget—accounts, income, bills, debts, payment history—lives encrypted on your device.

Here's how it works:

  • Encryption at rest: Your database is encrypted using AES-256, the same standard used by governments and financial institutions
  • Your key, your choice: The encryption key is generated on your machine and stored in the platform's secure store (Apple Keychain, Windows Credential Manager or Android Keystore). You control it.
  • No internet requirement: jBudget works completely offline. It never needs to connect to the internet to function.
  • Backups are encrypted too: If you export a backup file, it's encrypted with a passphrase you create. That file is useless without your passphrase.

What This Means for Your Privacy

With jBudget's encryption approach:

  • Only you can read your data. Even if someone gained physical access to your device, the encrypted database cannot be opened without your encryption key.
  • There is no server to breach. Your financial data isn't stored in a company's data center. It's on your machine.
  • No one is monetizing your budget. Your spending patterns, income, debt—none of it is mined for insights or analytics. It's yours alone.
  • You own the backups. When you export your data, you get an encrypted file you can store where you choose.

The Trade-Off You Make

This privacy comes with a practical cost: you manage your own backups. jBudget doesn't automatically backup to the cloud. If your device fails and you haven't made a manual backup, you lose your data.

For most people, this is the right trade: a few minutes of manual backup work, in exchange for complete privacy and control.

jBudget provides simple backup tools (one-click encrypted export, easy restore) and reminds you to back up regularly. But you're in control of whether and when that happens.

Encryption 101: What You Need to Know

AES-256: Advanced Encryption Standard with a 256-bit key. Even with modern computers, this would take longer than the age of the universe to brute-force. It's the security standard for financial institutions, government agencies, and military communications.

At rest vs. in transit: "At rest" means data sitting on your disk. "In transit" means data traveling across the internet. jBudget encrypts your data at rest (on your disk) and never transmits it, so there's no "in transit" exposure.

Your key vs. a master key: Some apps encrypt your data but hold the master key themselves (they can decrypt everything if they choose). jBudget's encryption key is yours alone, stored on your machine, never transmitted or shared.

What jBudget Can and Cannot Protect

jBudget protects:

  • Your budget data at rest on your device
  • Backups you export and encrypt with a passphrase

jBudget does NOT protect:

  • Your actual bank accounts (only your planning data about them)
  • Physical access to an unlocked device (jBudget doesn't require a password to open)
  • Malware on your device that might steal information while jBudget is running
  • Your internet usage (if your ISP logs which sites you visit)

jBudget protects your plan and your data. It does not protect against threats that exist outside the app.

Why This Approach Exists

jBudget was built by and for one person managing real family finances. The goal was straightforward: build a tool that couldn't lose your data, couldn't sell your data, and couldn't be compromised by someone else's security failure.

That's why it's local-first, encrypted, and offline by default. Your financial information is too important to trust to a third party.
