Mastering the Ledger: How to Confirm Payments and Stay on Top of Your Money

The Ledger screen is where your plan meets reality. Here's how to use it.

What Is the Ledger?

Your budget plan is a prediction: "Based on your accounts, income, bills, and debts, here's what I think will happen over the next 3 years."

But predictions are only useful if they track reality. That's what the Ledger is for. It's the record of what actually happened.

Every paycheck you receive, every bill you pay, every debt payment you make gets recorded in the Ledger. That record is what keeps your plan accurate and catches things that slip through the cracks.

The "To Confirm" Queue: Your Financial Checklist

The Ledger has multiple tabs. The first one—"To Confirm"—shows every scheduled payment that jBudget expects to happen. Income arriving, bills due, debt minimums owed.

Your job is simple: review the list and confirm what actually happened.

  • If the payment went through as scheduled: Tap it and mark it confirmed. Your account balance updates, and your plan adjusts.
  • If the payment didn't happen or was different: You'll see it in the queue as overdue, flagged for your attention.
  • If you sent more or less than the scheduled amount: Edit the entry to record the actual amount.

Why This Matters: The Bridge Between Plan and Reality

Imagine this scenario:

  • Your plan assumes a $200 credit card payment on the 15th of each month.
  • One month, you forget the payment. It doesn't go through.
  • Your actual account balance is $200 higher than the plan predicted.
  • Your plan still thinks you're on track, but you're not—that $200 payment is now overdue.

Without the Ledger, you wouldn't know the payment was missed until it's days late and late fees start piling up.

With the Ledger's "To Confirm" queue, the missed payment appears immediately. It sits in the queue as "past due," making it impossible to miss.

How to Use "To Confirm"

Step 1: Check the Queue Daily (or Weekly)

Open jBudget and tap the Ledger tab. Look at the "To Confirm" section. You'll see a list of:

  • Paychecks expected to arrive
  • Bills coming due
  • Debt minimum payments
  • Any custom income or transfer you set up

The older items are shown first. Anything past its due date appears at the top in red.

Step 2: Confirm What Actually Happened

For each item:

  • Check your bank account. Did the paycheck arrive? Did the bill post? Did the payment go through?
  • If it happened as planned: Tap the item and mark it confirmed.
  • If the amount was different: Tap it to edit. Change the amount to match your actual transaction.
  • If it hasn't happened yet but it's close: Leave it in the queue. Confirm it when it actually posts.

Step 3: Address Overdue Items

Items that are past due (in red) need attention:

  • If a payment actually happened late: Edit the entry to change the date to when it actually posted, then confirm.
  • If a payment still hasn't happened: It's now late and needs to be made immediately. Consider calling the creditor to explain.
  • If you simply can't make the payment yet: Leave it unconfirmed. jBudget already treats a past-due unconfirmed item as still owed, so your plan won't assume that money is spent until you actually confirm it once the payment goes through.

Real-World Example: Catching a Missed Bill

Say you have:

  • Checking account with $2,000
  • An insurance bill due on the 20th for $250
  • Expected paycheck on the 22nd for $2,500

You enter this into jBudget. The "To Confirm" queue shows:

  • Insurance bill due 20th: $250
  • Paycheck arriving 22nd: $2,500

On the 20th, you check your bank. The insurance payment hasn't posted yet. You mark it as pending in jBudget and move on.

On the 21st, you check again. Still nothing. The bill is now overdue and appears in red at the top of the "To Confirm" list.

You call the insurance company. Turns out there was a processing delay. They assure you it'll go through that day. You decide to wait.

On the 22nd, you check: the insurance payment finally posted, and your paycheck arrived too. You now confirm both in jBudget:

  • Insurance bill: Mark confirmed (it posted on the 21st)
  • Paycheck: Mark confirmed (it arrived as expected)

jBudget updates your balance: $2,000 - $250 (insurance) + $2,500 (paycheck) = $4,250. Your actual balance matches.

Your plan is now accurate again. If the late insurance payment affects your cash flow, the Plan tab shows you exactly how.

Why You Shouldn't Ignore the Queue

Some people treat the "To Confirm" queue as optional. They think: "I know my password. I can see my real bank balance. Why do I need to confirm things?"

The answer is simple: you can't be in two places at once.

Your bank knows your current balance. But your bank doesn't know your budget plan. It doesn't know that on day 10 of the month your account dips to $800, and on day 22 it jumps back up. Your bank doesn't know which expenses are essential and which are discretionary. It doesn't know your debt payoff timeline.

jBudget knows all of that. But only if you keep it in sync with your actual transactions. That's what confirming does.

Without confirmed transactions, your plan becomes fiction. It might say "you're on track," but that's only true if everything happened exactly as scheduled.

Tips for Staying Current

  • Check the queue weekly. Friday morning is a good rhythm: review the week that passed, confirm what happened, and see what's coming up.
  • Don't wait for things to be overdue. The moment a transaction posts in your bank, confirm it in jBudget. That keeps everything real-time.
  • Edit amounts when they don't match. If you sent a bigger payment than planned, jBudget should know. It helps with future cash flow planning.
  • Use bulk confirm on payday. If multiple items post at once (multiple paychecks, recurring bills), confirm them together.
  • Never ignore red (past-due) items. That's jBudget telling you something didn't happen as planned. Find out why.

History Tab: Seeing What You've Already Confirmed

Confirmed items move to the "History" tab. That's your permanent record of everything that actually happened. Use it to:

  • Verify patterns: Do your bills always post on the same day? Does your income fluctuate?
  • Track actual spending: How much did you really spend on groceries? On entertainment? The History shows every confirmed transaction.
  • Debug plan mismatches: If your plan predicts something different from what happened, the History is where you find the truth.

The Big Picture

Confirming payments in the Ledger is not busywork. It's the mechanism that keeps your plan honest. It's the reason jBudget can catch a forgotten bill before it becomes a late payment. It's why your payoff date is actually achievable, not a theoretical number from a calculator that doesn't know your real life.

Spend 5 minutes a week confirming your transactions. It's the difference between a plan that looks good and a plan that actually works.

Stay in control of your budget by confirming what actually happened. Get real visibility into your financial progress.
