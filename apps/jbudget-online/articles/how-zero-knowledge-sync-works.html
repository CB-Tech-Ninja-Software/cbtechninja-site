How zero-knowledge budget sync works

Syncing a household budget without letting the server read it: keys, ciphertext and what travels where.

The idea

“Zero-knowledge” here means the server holding your synced data can't read it. Your devices encrypt before sending and decrypt after receiving; the server never has the key.

The pieces

  • Local storage: each device keeps an encrypted database (SQLCipher, AES-256).
  • Keys: derived from a pairing phrase that never leaves your devices.
  • In transit and at rest on the relay: content is encrypted with AES-256-GCM before upload, so the relay stores and forwards only ciphertext.
  • Transport: our relay, or a hidden app-data folder in your own Google Drive.

What this protects against

If the relay's storage were stolen, the thieves would have ciphertext without the keys. The privacy policy puts it this way: your financial data would remain mathematically unreadable.

What it doesn't hide

Sync still has to know who is syncing. See what the server can and can't see.

A caution about the pairing phrase

Because keys come from the pairing phrase and never leave your devices, there is no server-side copy to recover them from. Keep the phrase somewhere safe.
