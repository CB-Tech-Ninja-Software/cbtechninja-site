How to resize a photo to under 100 KB or 200 KB

Application and ID photo uploads often ask for under 100 KB or 200 KB. Here is how to get a JPEG under that, and a warning about pixel dimensions.

A modern phone photo is 2 MB to 5 MB. An upload form that wants 100 KB or 200 KB is asking for a file 20 to 50 times smaller. That is possible for a photo, because JPEG compresses well, but it takes both lower pixel dimensions and stronger compression.

Two different limits: file size and pixel size

Many passport, visa and ID portals state both: a file size (say 200 KB) and pixel dimensions (say 600 by 600) or a particular ratio. Those are separate. The tools below make the file size smaller. FitSize works to a file-size limit only; it does not crop or resize to exact pixel dimensions. If your form needs exact dimensions, set those first with an editor, then bring the file down to the size limit.

Free ways to do it

  • Mac Preview. Open the photo, choose Tools > Adjust Size to lower the pixel dimensions, then File > Export as JPEG and drag the Quality slider. Preview shows the estimated file size as you drag.
  • Windows Photos or Paint. Resize to a smaller pixel size, then save as JPEG. Windows does not show a quality slider in Paint, so you may need to resize more than once.
  • iPhone. There is no built-in tool that targets a file size. Screenshots of a photo are not a good workaround, because they lower quality unpredictably.
  • HEIC photos. iPhone photos are often HEIC, which many portals do not accept. Convert to JPEG as part of shrinking.

A few tips for ID-style photos

  • Faces and plain backgrounds compress well. Busy backgrounds make bigger files.
  • Do not aim for 100 KB exactly. Aim a little under it so the upload does not fail on rounding.
  • Look at the result at full size. Over-compressed photos get blocky around the face.

Frequently asked questions

Does FitSize resize to exact pixel dimensions?

No. FitSize works to a file-size limit, not to exact pixel dimensions. It does scale a photo down when it needs to in order to reach the limit.

What format is the result?

Always JPEG. A photo that is already under your limit is left as it is.

Can I use a HEIC photo from my iPhone?

Yes. FitSize takes HEIC, JPEG and PNG and gives you a JPEG that fits.

Why is my file still over 100 KB?

Photos with lots of detail need more bytes. FitSize shows the smallest size it reached if the limit is too small. Try a slightly larger limit, or crop the photo to the face first so there is less to store.
