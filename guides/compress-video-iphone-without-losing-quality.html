How to compress a video on iPhone without losing much quality

iPhone videos get big quickly, especially 4K. Here is how to make them smaller while keeping them looking good.

A minute of 4K video from an iPhone can be several hundred MB. You rarely need that for sending a clip to a friend. Smaller files upload faster, fit messaging limits and use less storage, and for a phone screen most people will not notice the difference.

Shrink the next recording

In Settings > Camera > Record Video, choose 1080p HD at 30 fps instead of 4K at 60 fps. The files are much smaller. Under Settings > Camera > Formats, “High Efficiency” uses a modern codec that makes smaller files than “Most Compatible”, at the cost of compatibility with older devices.

Shrink a video you already have

  • Trim it. Photos > select the video > Edit > drag the handles. Shorter always means smaller.
  • Mail. When you attach a video in the Mail app, it can offer smaller size options before sending.
  • Messages and AirDrop. These send the original or a compressed version depending on the app and options, so the result can vary.

None of these let you say “make it 15 MB”. They give you steps, and you find out the result afterwards.

How much quality do you lose?

Every re-encode loses some detail, but a sensible one is hard to spot on a phone. The size you choose matters more than the tool. A 30-second clip at 16 MB looks fine; the same size for 5 minutes looks soft.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a built-in way to compress a video on iPhone?

Not one that targets a size. You can lower recording settings, trim a clip, or use the size options when attaching in Mail.

What does the output look like?

It is an MP4 with H.264 video and AAC audio. How good it looks depends mostly on the size you ask for compared with the clip length.
