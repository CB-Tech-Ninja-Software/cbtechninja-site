How to reduce a PDF to under 1 MB or 2 MB for an upload form

Visa, school and government forms commonly cap uploads at 1 MB or 2 MB. Here is how to get a PDF under the limit without wrecking it.

Many official portals cap each upload at 1 MB, 2 MB or 5 MB. A phone scan of a few pages can easily be 8 MB. The good news is that 1 MB to 2 MB is a comfortable target: most documents can reach it and stay readable.

Read the rule carefully

Look at what the form actually says: the maximum for one file, or for all files together? Does it want one PDF or allow several? Is it asking for KB or MB? 1 MB is 1,000 KB, and some systems count 1 MB as 1,048,576 bytes. If you aim for a little under the stated number, you are safe either way.

Free ways to shrink a PDF

  • Scan smarter. Phone scanning apps and document scanners let you choose a quality setting. Greyscale at 150 to 200 dpi is usually enough for forms and typically halves the size or better.
  • Export again from the source. From Word, choose Save As PDF with “Minimum size”. From Pages, Export to PDF and choose a lower image quality.
  • Mac Preview. File > Export, then the “Reduce File Size” Quartz filter. It can work for 2 MB, but results vary and it can make images look rough.
  • Combine carefully. If you merge several scans into one PDF, shrink each scan first.

Keep it readable

Before uploading, open the smaller file and zoom in on stamps, signatures and small print. If something is hard to read, aim for a slightly larger limit. Officials reject unreadable documents just as they reject oversized ones.

Frequently asked questions

What size should I aim for if the limit is 2 MB?

A little under, such as 1.8 MB. FitSize uses decimal units (1 MB is 1,000,000 bytes), so a file under 2 MB is under 2 MB by either common way of counting.

Does compressing a PDF change the page size or page count?

Not with FitSize. Page count and page size stay the same.

Can I do this on my phone?

Yes. FitSize also runs on iPhone and iPad. On iPhone, a shrunk PDF becomes page images, so its text will not be selectable.

What if the PDF will not go under the limit?

FitSize tells you plainly and shows the smallest size it reached, instead of giving you an unreadable file. Split the document into parts if the portal allows several uploads, or ask for a higher limit.
