How to make a PDF smaller than 200 KB

Job applications and online portals often cap a PDF at 200 KB. Here is how to get under it, what you give up, and the quickest way.

200 KB is small. A one-page CV that is mostly text is usually well under it. A scanned page or a PDF with photos in it is often 1 MB to 5 MB, so you have to shrink it. The size almost always comes from images, not text.

Check first that the limit really is 200 KB (200,000 bytes) and not 2 MB, which is ten times bigger and much easier to hit.

Why your PDF is bigger than it looks

Text costs almost nothing in a PDF. Scans, photos, logos and screenshots are where the megabytes come from. A scan made at 300 dpi in colour can be 1 MB or more per page. If your document is a scan, the fix is to make the page images smaller, either lower resolution, more compression, or both.

Free ways to do it

  • Re-export from the source. If you have the Word or Pages file, export again as PDF. In Word on Windows, Save As PDF and choose “Minimum size (publishing online)”. This works well for documents with a few images.
  • Mac Preview. Open the PDF, choose File > Export, and pick the “Reduce File Size” Quartz filter. It is a blunt tool: it often makes pages blurry, and sometimes the file barely shrinks. Always check the result.
  • Re-scan. If you scan the paper yourself, scan in greyscale at 150 dpi instead of colour at 300 dpi. The file will be a fraction of the size and is still readable for most forms.
  • Split or trim. Remove pages the portal does not need.

The catch with all of these is that you are guessing. You export, look at the file size, and try again until it is under 200 KB.

What you give up at 200 KB

To get a multi-page scan under 200 KB, the pages usually have to become low-resolution images. Text stays readable if the original scan was clear, but fine print and stamps can go soft. Look at every page at 100% zoom before you upload. If the portal needs selectable text (for example, it reads details out of your CV), tell the app or tool to keep text where it can, and ask the portal whether a larger file is allowed.

Frequently asked questions

What if my PDF is still over 200 KB after compressing?

Some documents simply cannot reach a limit that small without becoming unreadable, for example a long multi-page colour scan. FitSize says so and shows the smallest size it reached. Try a smaller page count, re-scan in greyscale, or ask the portal for a higher limit.

Will the text still be selectable?

Often yes, when the size comes from images and recompressing them is enough. If FitSize has to rebuild pages as images to reach a small target, text in the smaller copy cannot be selected or searched, and the result screen tells you.

Is 200 KB the same as 0.2 MB?

Yes. 200 KB is 200,000 bytes, or 0.2 MB, using the decimal units most upload forms and FitSize use.

Is it safe to use an online PDF compressor?

Online compressors need you to upload the file to someone else's server. For CVs, IDs and tax forms, a tool that works on your own device avoids that question altogether.
